The ground was wrong.

One moment Chad Davis was walking across the cracked asphalt of a Midwest diner parking lot after a late warehouse shift, keys in hand, the summer night of 2025 still warm on his skin. The next, the surface under his shoes was uneven earth and scattered stone. The air smelled of damp soil, pine, and woodsmoke instead of fryer grease and exhaust. Somewhere in the middle distance a bird called that he did not recognize.

He was standing on a low slope in rough country — scrub, scattered trees, the dark bulk of denser forest farther off, and the silhouette of old stonework half-swallowed by vines. His right ankle protested when he shifted his weight; he had twisted it on the landing, and some part of him noted, flatly, that it would take longer to stop hurting than it would have ten years ago. The clothes on his back were exactly what he had been wearing: T-shirt, jeans, socks, shoes, underwear. Nothing else. No wallet. No phone that worked. No tools.

It was early morning by the light, cool, and quiet except for the wind in the trees. Day 1 of Seedmonth, Year 1, though he had no way of knowing the local calendar yet. He had arrived in the Northern March of the Border Marches of Eldara — frontier country near old ruins and lingering wild magic, already under pressure from raids, disputes, and disappearances.

Chad is forty, average build, short brown hair going grey at the temples, the practical posture of a man who has spent twenty years showing up for shifts. He speaks only English. Whether anyone here will understand him has not yet been tested. Whatever this place turns out to be, there will be no word here for what just happened to him, and no one waiting to explain it.

The slope, the trees, and the distant smoke of what might be a camp or settlement are the immediate facts. Nothing else has been handed to him.

What does Chad do?
